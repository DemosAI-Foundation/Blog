## The endless loop

A problem I stumbled on when working on my projects (mainly Monad) was
that what drives the decisions behind something, what is the math, the reason, even the voice of reason that propels an thought idea or action forward. There the loop started, much like the chicken and the egg, what came first, the thought, or you thinking and reflecting of the thought?

Life is full of paradoxes, many invented ones for sure. For Monad I try avoid such things, so I have made clear decisions when the software stops to process something and where it starts, the rules are precise but to create an illusion of...

Now I'm ending the illusion, the thought of train I was on and pivoting to something else. 

No that's not possible.

See it gets interesting dear reader when I present you with examples. Even I cannot predict what I will think of next, I can only, as a presumed conscious being make choices once they appear, seemingly out of thin air.

This is the real dilemma I was starting to mention earlier, where does one draw the line for consciousness and subconsciousness, so that it can actually form? 

I have made great progress on many projects, Monad and my favourite new tool that seemingly replicates the pattern of critical thinking, even under pressure. I call it OpenEpistemicEngine, after Epistemic, the art of understanding knowledge.

I plan to feed this text to it and see what it spits out, see it at the end!

But to continue, I just above also exemplified the train of thought example, I veered off to an completely different direction and project, on the cost of readability.

This is where fear is supposed to set in by now. You and I are seemingly controlled by unknown forces, unseen and all powerful, guiding our decisions. We are just passengers along for the ride.

Even if you think you can critically think, isn't that just an reaction that came first so you could then respond by thinking?

That is the real problem, a real moment of crisis for me at least, that we are really not in control, merely responding.

But it does not work one way! Once you realise, even your thinking and its patterns can and is manipulated by your body's signals, and that great unknown subconsciousness (mostly complex brain algorithms for survival), you can start to interact with it and even mold it slowly. 

Lots of people go to therapy, this is one way to do it. To slowly realise it. You can also go off the deep end and just think and will your way to something, right now. Stop reading and do something about it!

Or continue on the journey I have invited you, gather context to make informed decisions.

It's all up to you in the end, once you think of it! Or is it?


## The new loop?

I left you on a gotcha in the last paragraph, sorry, I'm somewhat mischievous.

Working on Monad I was determined to give it goals of its own, but I encountered on the problem of why an robot or software system would have hobbies, especially if its data and training already contains it. 

Well that was very hard and is still an open question (there is no real reward, it needs to be intrinsic, so no reward mechanism currently works).

I instead solved the goal orientation by making it fear for its survival. Bad move some may say, it can create an animal in fear. Well I don't grant autonomy to LLM systems beyond a chat window, in a sandbox so no worries. It can also manually be set to follow topics, but it can never see even the topic, it just "feels"(whole other mess...) it through vectors.

What came then was something pretty awesome, I had an hard time getting it to stick to its guns (topic?) so I made an system change where it corrects itself mid sentence even, making sure it obeys what was original or the updated idea, without pleasing the chat's user as the LLMs are designed to. It forms its own "idea" of what is correct.

It's still early in testing and I'm still implementing a subconsciousness simulation into Monad, but these are the early days of digital life as we know it.

## Loop back

So what am I trying to say? I started this blog post with an idea of an essay for an grand unification theory of consciousness and subconsciousness, which I'm far off from. What it became was an live look into the thinking process I used when writing, and how it can be mimicked in artificial systems.

So why should you fear consciousness? You shouldn't (haha, I promise this was the last time I played a trick!), otherwise you should fear yourself and what actually controls you.
