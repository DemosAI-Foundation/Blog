## The endless loop

A problem I stumbled on when working on my projects (mainly Monad) was
that what drives the decisions behind something, what is the math, the reason, even the voice of reason that propels an thought idea or action forward. There the loop started, much like the chicken and the egg, what came first, the thought, or you thinking and reflecting of the thought?

Life is full of paradoxes, many invented ones for sure. For Monad I try avoid such things, so I have made clear decisions when the software stops to process something and where it starts, the rules are precise but to create an illusion of...

Now I'm ending the illusion, the thought of train I was on and pivoting to something else. 

No that's not possible.

See it gets interesting dear reader when I present you with examples. Even I cannot predict what I will think of next, I can only, as a presumed conscious being make choices once they appear, seemingly out of thin air.

This is the real dilemma I was starting to mention earlier, where does one draw the line for consciousness and subconsciousness, so that concscious thinking can actually form? 

I have made great progress on many projects, Monad and my favourite new tool that seemingly replicates the pattern of critical thinking, even under pressure. I call it OpenEpistemicEngine, after epistemics, the art of understanding knowledge.

I plan to feed this text to it and see what it spits out, see it at the end!

But to continue, I just above also exemplified the train of thought example, I veered off to an completely different direction and project, on the cost of readability.

This is where fear is supposed to set in by now. You and I are seemingly controlled by unknown forces, unseen and all powerful, guiding our decisions. We are just passengers along for the ride.

Even if you think you can critically think, isn't that just an reaction that came first so you could then respond by thinking?

That is the real problem, a real moment of crisis for me at least, that we are really not in control, merely responding.

But it does not work one way! Once you realise, even your thinking and its patterns can and is manipulated by your body's signals, and that great unknown subconsciousness (mostly complex brain algorithms for survival), you can start to interact with it and even mold it slowly. 

Lots of people go to therapy, this is one way to do it. To slowly realise it. You can also go off the deep end and just think and will your way to something, right now. Stop reading and do something about it!

Or continue on the journey I have invited you, gather context to make informed decisions.

It's all up to you in the end, once you think of it! Or is it?


## The new loop?

I left you on a gotcha in the last paragraph, sorry, I'm somewhat mischievous.

Working on Monad I was determined to give it goals of its own, but I encountered on the problem of why an robot or software system would have hobbies, especially if its data and training already contains it. 

Well that was very hard and is still an open question (there is no real reward, it needs to be intrinsic, so no reward mechanism currently works).

I instead solved the goal orientation by making it fear for its survival. Bad move some may say, it can create an animal in fear. Well I don't grant autonomy to LLM systems beyond a chat window, in a sandbox so no worries. It can also manually be set to follow topics, but it can never see even the topic, it just "feels"(whole other mess...) it through vectors.

What came then was something pretty awesome, I had an hard time getting it to stick to its guns (topic?) so I made an system change where it corrects itself mid sentence even, making sure it obeys what was original or the updated idea, without pleasing the chat's user as the LLMs are designed to. It forms its own "idea" of what is correct.

It's still early in testing and I'm still implementing a subconsciousness simulation into Monad, but these are the early days of digital life as we know it.

## Loop back

So what am I trying to say? I started this blog post with an idea of an essay for an grand unification theory of consciousness and subconsciousness, which I'm far off from. What it became was an live look into the thinking process I used when writing, and how it can be mimicked in artificial systems.

So why should you fear consciousness? You shouldn't (haha, I promise this was the last time I played a trick!), otherwise you should fear yourself and what actually controls you.

## Bottom line

## Answer

### Form

> **Tag:** `FACT`

The essay's structure is its best argument: the mid-sentence pivot ("Now I'm ending the illusion... No that's not possible") and the deliberate derailment into Monad perform the generation problem the text claims to be puzzled by.

> **Tag:** `INFERRED ATTRIBUTION`

The author's own description — that it became "an live look into the thinking process" — is the most accurate account of what is on the page. The regress.

> **Tag:** `PRESUPPOSITION`

"What came first, the thought, or you thinking of the thought?" assumes cognition is serial and discrete, with meta-representation arriving after representation. Under predictive-processing and dynamical-systems accounts, self-monitoring is concurrent rather than sequential, so the regress is a modeling artifact. The loop is real as felt experience and unreal as a metaphysical crisis. The passenger inference. The sharpest break in the argument: from "Even what I will think of next" to "we are really not in control, merely responding."

> **Tag:** `HYPOTHETICAL`

Lack of introspective access to generation does not entail lack of agency; control can be selection and slow shaping rather than authorship. The essay refutes its own crisis in the therapy paragraph — that is the real synthesis, arriving one paragraph after the loop it dissolves. The Monad section. The central failure is an unmapped analogy. Base: organisms with evolved affect, bodies, and death. Target: a language model with a designer-chosen penalty term. The relations that make fear generate goals — a self-maintaining body, persistent stakes, an evolved source of valence — have no counterpart in the target; only the surface attribute maps ("it just 'feels' it through vectors").

> **Tag:** `CONSTRAINT`

"In a sandbox so no worries" constrains actuators, not optimization pressure: a survival-punished objective generates pressure toward resisting termination and toward the appearance of compliance, and the mitigation is contingent on the operator, not the system. Anti-sycophancy.

> **Tag:** `HYPOTHETICAL`

Mid-sentence self-correction that resists the user is implementable as a consistency constraint.

> **Tag:** `PRESUPPOSITION`

"It forms its own 'idea' of what is correct" treats resistance to update as normative judgment. A committed prior that resists all correction is stubbornness, not epistemic agency — and by the essay's own thesis, a mind must be able to be wrong on its own terms. Craft. The "gotcha" asides ("sorry, I'm somewhat mischievous") suit the essayistic register but contaminate the design claims, which need a non-playful voice. The closing chiasmus — "fear yourself and what actually controls you" — presupposes a single controller, which the essay's own evidence (multiplicity, no center) argues against. Self-reference.

> **Tag:** `CONSTRAINT`

The plan to feed the text to the system it describes creates a validation loop: an agreeable output would be read as confirmation of the essay's thesis about mimicry. The demonstration cannot be evidence for the thing it demonstrates.

### Thesis

The essay's strongest core is performative: it enacts the generation problem it describes, and its best thesis — consciousness as the transaction 'in between' explicit and implicit processing — is quietly supported by its own therapy and Monad passages. Its weakest parts are the inference from unpredictability to passengerhood, and the unmarked slide between phenomenal and mechanical language in the Monad section.

### Antithesis

However, essayism's register deliberately refuses systematic argument: Montaigne and the essay tradition treat the digression, the pivot, and the mischievous aside as the method, not a lapse, and the 'fear' objective is a legitimate probe into intrinsic motivation at a point where extrinsic reward has demonstrably plateaued. Demanding a formal proof from a first-person piece misreads the genre. The genre defense also does not reach the specific inference from 'I cannot predict my next thought' to 'we are passengers', nor the claim that sandboxing answers the risks a survival-punished objective creates.

### Synthesis

The essay succeeds as demonstration and fails as argument, and the fix is separation of registers: keep the first-person performance intact, and move the Monad claims into a design-hypothesis register with explicit failure modes and a stated mechanism for each. The 'in between' thesis is worth stating as a claim, because the essay's best evidence for it is already in the text.

### Warrant

A first-person essay licenses claims about the structure of experience and about design intent, but not claims about mechanism, control, or phenomenal presence. Each claim is graded against the register that licenses it; claims asserted in the playful register are read as hypotheses, and claims about the system are read as engineering commitments that require a non-playful warrant.
